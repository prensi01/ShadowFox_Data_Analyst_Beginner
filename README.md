Superstore Sales Analysis — ShadowFox Data Analyst Internship
📌 Project Overview
This project is completed as part of the ShadowFox Data Analyst Internship – Beginner Level.

The objective of this project is to analyze a Superstore sales dataset and identify important business trends related to sales, profit, quantity, orders, categories, products, and monthly performance.

The analysis was performed using Microsoft Excel, including data cleaning, PivotTables, calculations, charts, and a business dashboard.

🎯 Project Objectives
The main objectives of this project are:

Clean and organize the sales dataset.
Calculate important business KPIs.
Analyze monthly sales trends.
Analyze sales and profit by category.
Identify top-performing products.
Identify loss-making products.
Calculate category-level profit margins.
Create charts and visual summaries.
Build an Excel business dashboard.
Generate meaningful business insights.
Provide practical business recommendations.

🧹 Data Cleaning
The following data-cleaning activities were performed:

1. Converted Dataset into Excel Table
The complete dataset was converted into an Excel Table to make filtering, sorting, formulas, and analysis easier.

2. Duplicate Check
Duplicate records were checked using Excel's Remove Duplicates feature.

No duplicate values were found in the dataset.

3. Missing Value Check
Important columns were checked for blank values, including:

Order Date
Customer ID
Customer Name
Region
Category
Product Name
Sales
Quantity
Profit
No missing values were found in these key columns.

4. Order Date Cleaning
The Order Date column contained dates in different formats.

A separate Clean Order Date column was created to standardize the date values and make them suitable for PivotTable-based monthly analysis.

# 🔎 Key Business Insights

### 1. Overall Performance

The business generated approximately **2.33 million in total sales** and **292.30K in total profit**.

### 2. Technology Category Performance

Technology generated the highest sales of **839,893.28** and the highest profit of **146,543.38** among the three categories.

The category achieved a profit margin of **17.45%**.

### 3. Office Supplies Performance

Office Supplies generated **731,893.31 in sales** and **126,023.44 in profit**.

The category achieved a profit margin of **17.22%**.

### 4. Furniture Performance

Furniture generated **754,747.76 in sales**, but its profit was only **19,729.99**.

Its profit margin was **2.61%**.

### 5. Product Performance

The **Canon imageCLASS 2200 Advanced Copier** was the highest-sales product, generating **61,599.82 in sales** and **25,199.93 in profit**.

### 6. Loss-Making Products

Some products among the top-selling products recorded negative profit.

Examples include:

- Cisco TelePresence System EX90 Videoconferencing Unit: **-1,811.08 profit**
- GBC DocuBind P400 Electric Binding System: **-1,878.17 profit**
- High Speed Automatic Electric Letter Opener: **-262.00 profit**

These products may require further analysis of pricing, discounts, and costs.

### 7. Overall Profitability

The overall profit margin of the analyzed dataset was **12.56%**.

# 💡 Business Recommendations

Based on the analysis, the following recommendations were identified:

### 1. Monitor Technology Products

Technology contributes strongly to both sales and profit. High-performing Technology products should be monitored regularly.

### 2. Investigate Furniture Profitability

Furniture generates substantial sales but comparatively lower profit.

The business should review:

- Product pricing
- Discounts
- Product costs
- Individual product margins

### 3. Review Loss-Making Products

Products generating negative profit should be reviewed individually to understand whether pricing, discounts, costs, or other factors are contributing to the losses.

### 4. Monitor High-Sales Products

High-sales products should be evaluated not only on revenue but also on profitability to ensure that strong sales contribute positively to overall business performance.

### 5. Use Monthly Sales Trends

Monthly sales analysis can help identify periods of relatively higher and lower sales and can support future sales planning and inventory management.
